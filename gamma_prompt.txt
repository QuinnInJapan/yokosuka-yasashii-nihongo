## Background

やさしい日本語 ("easy Japanese") is simplified Japanese used to communicate with foreign residents, elderly people, and others who struggle with standard administrative language. Many municipalities are adopting it, but staff find the rewriting process difficult because:

- There's no single correct answer — the same word can be rewritten differently depending on context and audience
- Debates over whether/how to rewrite a term eat up meeting time
- Different departments in the same city hall rewrite the same words differently
- Staff can't explain *why* they chose a particular rewriting — there's no evidence to point to

## What the tool does

It collects precedents: real rewriting examples from 19 government bodies and academic institutions across Japan. Staff type a word into an Excel sheet and instantly see:

- JLPT difficulty level (N1–N5)
- A recommendation based on how often that word gets rewritten across all sources (e.g., "almost always rewritten" / "usually kept as-is")
- Specific term-level rewriting examples with source attribution
- Sentence-level before/after examples

The approach is: ① collect rewriting examples from published sources → ② organize into a searchable database with auto-calculated difficulty and rewrite rates → ③ make it available in a single Excel file, no installation needed.

Key message: this tool doesn't replace human judgment — it provides the evidence to support it.

## Data sources

The tool draws on 3 categories of data. The audience likely doesn't know what a "corpus" is, so this needs a brief, accessible explanation:

A corpus (コーパス) is a large, systematically collected body of text used for language research. This tool uses parallel corpora — paired collections of original text and its やさしい日本語 rewrite.

**行政ガイドライン (Government guidelines)**
- Created by: staff at municipalities and national agencies
- Content: term-level rewriting examples (e.g., 避難 → にげる)
- Scale: 17 institutions, 2,800+ term pairs
- Key trait: based on official administrative judgment

**MATCHA コーパス (MATCHA corpus)**
- Created by: Ehime University + professional writers at MATCHA (a travel media company)
- Content: sentence-level rewrites of travel/culture articles
- Scale: 14,700+ sentence pairs
- Key trait: written by professional writers for real readers

**JADES データセット (JADES dataset)**
- Created by: a single expert at NAIST (Nara Institute of Science and Technology)
- Content: sentence-level rewrites of news articles
- Scale: 3,900+ sentence pairs
- Key trait: simplifies both vocabulary and grammar

## How search works

Example: user types「避難」
- Difficulty: N2
- Recommendation: ほぼいつも言い換えられている (almost always rewritten)
- Term hits: 8 matches
- Sentence hits: 24 matches
- Top term result: 避難 → にげる — found in 5 sources (入管庁, 川崎市, 札幌市…)

## How sentence examples work

Two example pairs:
- 地震が発生した場合は、速やかに避難してください。→ 地震が　おきたら、すぐ　にげて　ください。
- 本届出は、転入の届出と同時にすることができます。→ この届出は、引っ越しの届出と一緒に出すことができます。

## How the recommendation system works

Based on rewrite rate = (times rewritten in sentence examples) ÷ (total sentence examples containing the word):
- 80%+ → ほぼいつも言い換えられている
- 50–79% → 過半数で言い換えられている
- 20–49% → たまに言い換えられている
- Below 20% → 大体そのまま使われている

Important caveat: the recommendation is reference information only. Final decisions are made by staff based on context.

## Source credibility

17 government sources: 出入国在留管理庁・文化庁、厚生労働省、自治体国際化協会、川崎市、横浜市、札幌市、福岡市、大阪市、大阪府、京都市、愛知県、三重県、鳥取県、岡山県、入間市

3 academic sources: 弘前大学（災害基礎語彙集成）、愛媛大学（MATCHAパラレルコーパス）、奈良先端科学技術大学院大学（JADESデータセット）

Key insight: when multiple independent sources adopt the same rewriting, consensus is high → reliability is high.

## Benefits

- Faster decisions — look up precedents instantly instead of debating from scratch
- Defensible choices — "Kawasaki, Sapporo, and 3 other cities use the same rewriting"
- Consistency — different departments can reference the same data
- Evidence-based — decisions grounded in actual rewrite rates, not intuition
